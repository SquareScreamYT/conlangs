# Rose Elder

**Sambucus rosea** (Isterian: *rystek*), also known as rose elder,
is a species of shrub in the moschatel family Viburnaceae.
It is native to boreal forests and produces small, red berries.
The plant is largely poisonous, except for the flowers and fruit.
It is widely grown as an ornamental shrub or small tree.

Both the flowers and the berries have a long tradition of culinary use, primarily for syrups, jams and tea.
It is culturally significant to the Isterians, where it is the national flower.

## Description

The rose elder is a deciduous shrub or small tree often growing to 2-4 meters tall; in some cases, trees can reach up to 6m tall.
They typically live for up to 120 years, taking approximately 10 years to reach maturity.
The bark, light yellowish-grey when young, changes to a coarse yellowish-grey outer bark with deep lengthwise furrowing, lenticels prominent.
The leaves are arranged in opposite pairs, 10-30cm long, pinnate with five to seven (rarely nine) leaflets,
the leaflets 5-10 cm long and 3-5cm broad, with a serrated margin.
The young stems are hollow.

The hermaphroditic flowers have four stamens, which are borne in large, flat corymbs 8-20 cm in diameter in late spring to mid-summer.
The individual flowers range from rose to ivory-white, 6-8 millimeters in diameter, with four petals,
and produce a strong scent resembling lychee which attracts pollinating flies, beetles, bees, bats, hummingbirds and butterflies.

The fruit is a bright red drupe containing 3 to 5 seeds, produced in druping clusters in late autumn.
It is eaten by birds, which disperse the seeds in their droppings.
The rich colour results from their anthocyanin content.
The fruit and flowers contain a low amount of cyanogenic glycosides and sambunigrin compared to the leaves, seeds, branches and sap.

## Use

The bright red berries are edible though with an mild, sweet flavour when fully ripe, but are tart when immature.
They are traditionally used to make jam, syrups, jelly and wine.
The seeds are removed by passing the purée through a fine cloth sieve.

Commonly, the flowerheads are used to make syrups and tea. Branches of flowers are also used to make bouquets.
The rose elder is the national flower of Chielbary. In Isterian culture, a single sprig, often accompanied by lilies of the valley,
left on an enemy's doorstep is a death threat,
however, when paired with other flowers such as lilacs, lavenders, violets and roses, are a symbol of spring and love.
